# The app's own words

Red Moon measures a handful of things about your practice, and it uses six words for them that it
means quite precisely. None of them is a standard music term, which is exactly why they are written
down: *mastery* and *command tempo* in particular are two different questions about the same loop,
and mixing them up is the fastest way to make the numbers useless to you.

Everywhere one of these appears in the app it carries a small **ⓘ**
<!-- shot: terms/info-button | role: glyph | alt: -->
next to its label. Tap it and the definition appears in a popover. The definitions below are those
same popovers, word for word — if the two ever differ, the app is right and this page is stale.

For scales, modes, intervals, chord names and the rest of the musical vocabulary, go to
**Toolkit ▸ Glossary**, which is where all of that is defined and searchable. This page covers only
the words Red Moon coined for its own practice model.

## Mastery

> How cleanly you own this loop — feel, tone, accuracy. Separate from speed: you can play something fast but scrappy, or slow but perfect. A rating is kept with the tempo you gave it at, so it still means something after you move on.

**Where you set it.** Open a song, hold a loop in the **Loops** panel to open its edit sheet, and
set **Mastery** there. It is a rating out of five and it is yours to set — nothing in Red Moon
listens to you play or assigns it for you.

An untouched loop shows no rating at all rather than a zero. That is deliberate: a loop you have
never assessed and a loop you have assessed as poor are different things, and only one of them
should look like a problem.

**A rating remembers the tempo you gave it at.** Under the rating you will see a line like
*Rated at 85%* — on an exercise, *Rated at 90 BPM · 8ths*. Once you move the command tempo past
that, the line adds *command has moved since*: your rating is still yours and still stands, but
it describes a speed you have since left behind.

That matters most when you raise your command tempo straight after rating something 5. Without
the tempo attached, a 5 would take the drill out of rotation entirely — at a speed you had not
actually rated it at yet. With it, the drill comes back round instead, so you get a chance to
say whether the new tempo is really clean.

## Command tempo

> The fastest speed you own this loop at, as a % of the original. Command is speed; Mastery is cleanliness — deliberately two axes.

**Where you set it.** The same loop edit sheet, under **Command tempo**. It is expressed as a
percentage of the song's original speed, so 100% means you have it at the tempo the record is at.

Because the two axes are independent, a loop can sit at a high command tempo and a low mastery —
you can get through it at full speed, but it is scrappy. That combination is a useful thing to be
able to see, and it is the reason the app refuses to average them into a single score.

## Focus

> Your intent right now, not how well you play it. Backburner (parked) · Active (in rotation) · Sharpening (pushing it / gig prep).

**Where you set it.** The loop edit sheet again, under **Focus** — and on several loops at once
from the bulk edit sheet, which you reach by holding a panel header to start selecting and then
choosing more than one loop.

Focus is the field that answers "what am I working on *this month*", so it is the one worth
revisiting when your practice changes direction. Mastery and command tempo describe where you are;
focus describes where you are pointing.

## Type

> What kind of loop: Lick (melodic) · Riff (melody + rhythm) · Chords (rhythmic) · Passage (a longer span covering several).

**Where you set it.** The loop edit sheet, under **Type**, and in the bulk edit sheet.

Type is descriptive, not functional — nothing in the app behaves differently because a loop is a
riff rather than a lick. It exists so that a library of eighty loops can still be sorted into things
you recognise.

## Mastery, on a song

> Averaged from this song's loops — rate individual loops to set it. “Unrated” until at least one loop has a mastery.

**Where you find it.** Hold a song's title in the player, or open the song's details from the
library, and **Mastery** appears among its details. You cannot set it directly — it is derived, and
the way to move it is to rate the loops underneath it.

## Your command tempo, on an exercise

> The fastest you can play it cleanly and repeatably right now. The warm-up floor and the reach derive from it — tune them when you run the drill. Command is speed, separate from how cleanly you own it.

**Where you set it.** When you configure an exercise, under the **Your command tempo** heading.
This is the exercise-side twin of a loop's command tempo, and it does more work: the drill's
starting tempo and the tempo it reaches for are both derived from the number you put here.

---

## What Red Moon does not measure

There is no score, no grade and no assessment of your playing anywhere in the app — by design, and
permanently. Mastery is a number you choose, not a number you are given.
